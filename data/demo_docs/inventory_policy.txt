SMART RETAIL - SAMPLE INVENTORY POLICY

1. Reorder planning
The store should review replenishment needs at least once per day. A reorder recommendation should consider forecast demand during the supplier lead time and a separate safety-stock buffer. The basic reorder point is expected demand during lead time plus safety stock.

2. Dairy and chilled products
Milk, eggs and other chilled products must be stored in the appropriate refrigerated area. Staff should rotate stock using the first-expiry-first-out (FEFO) principle. Products close to their expiry date should be highlighted for review and markdown according to store policy.

3. Supplier lead time
Each product should have a supplier lead time recorded in the inventory system. If the supplier lead time changes, the inventory record should be updated before the next reorder calculation.

4. Promotions
Promotional periods can increase demand. The forecasting process should include discount and promotion information where available. Store managers should review model forecasts before placing unusually large orders.

5. Model limitations
Demand forecasts are estimates based on historical patterns. They may be less reliable during new product launches, major promotions, supply interruptions or unusual events. A manager must review forecasts alongside current stock, supplier constraints and local knowledge.

6. Data quality
Sales records should have a valid product, sale date, units sold and selling price. Missing dates should be treated carefully; a date with no transaction should not automatically be interpreted as missing data unless the source system confirms that the store was open and data collection was operational.
